\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Realised stock histories mix the properties of scientific advice with the
degree to which that advice was followed. We backtest ICES Category~1 MSY
advice for six Northeast Atlantic stocks, comparing the latest WGCSE
assessment with two perfect-information counterfactuals on the same
Operating Model biology: open-loop fishing at constant ICES SAG
$\Fmsy$ ($\Ftar$), and the ICES hockey-stick harvest control rule
(Advice rule) with true Operating Model SSB and no implementation error.
Results are organised as Historical, Current and Future. Both windows use the
\texttt{bh3} Beverton--Holt scenario ($R_0$ prior from a hockey-stick);
ICES harvest-control-rule parameters remain the SAG values attached to
\texttt{bh1} (unconstrained \texttt{bevholtSV}, estimated steepness). All six stocks
sit below $\Btrig$ in the terminal year. Constant $\Ftar$ rebuilds Irish
Sea whiting and Celtic Sea cod well above the trigger; Irish Sea cod
ends lower than history because realised $F$ is already far below
$\Ftar$. Celtic Sea whiting never reaches ICES $\Btrig$ within 20~years,
yet reaches Operating Model $\Bmsy$ in 16--18~years. Remaining
stock--recruitment scenarios and an advised-catch trajectory are
forthcoming.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textit{Keywords:} ICES advice; harvest control rule; backtest;
$F_{\mathrm{MSY}}$; stock--recruitment; rebuilding.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fisheries management advice is judged, in practice, against a single
realised history: the sequence of assessed stock states and catches that
actually occurred. That history is the joint product of the scientific
advice rule and the extent to which managers and fleets implemented it.
A stock that is depleted at the end of a management period may have been
driven there by a poorly performing harvest control rule (HCR), by
systematic departure from the advised catch, or by some combination of
the two. Distinguishing these explanations matters for where corrective
effort should be directed. Standard retrospective and hindcast
diagnostics applied to stock assessments do not, by themselves, separate
advice quality from advice compliance.

The intended design of this study \citep{ices2023advice} is a backtest of
ICES Category~1 MSY advice: reconstruct the perception of stock status
in each historical year, apply the advice rule then in force, and
project the Operating Model under perfect implementation of the
resulting catch advice. Comparing that counterfactual with realised
history isolates implementation from the advice rule. Performance is
summarised in three linked components used throughout: \textbf{Historical}
(the hindcast window), \textbf{Current} (terminal status), and
\textbf{Future} (a forward projection from each terminal state). Every
metric is reported for the realised trajectory and for the
counterfactuals, together with their difference.

This draft implements a restricted version of that design. Operating
models are conditioned on the latest Working Group for the Celtic Seas
Ecoregion (WGCSE) age-structured assessments. Two harvest policies are
then applied with perfect information on the Operating Model: a simple
open-loop projection at constant ICES SAG $\Fmsy$ from 2006
($F_{\mathrm{target}}$; no feedback), and a closed-loop MSE with
feedback control from 2005 (Advice rule: the ICES hockey-stick revises
$F$ from SSB each year). The Advice rule uses true Operating Model SSB
(\texttt{err = NULL}), zero implementation error, and a one-year advice
lag. It is not a contemporaneous peel of historical ICES catch advice,
and it is not an estimation-error management strategy evaluation. An
advised-catch trajectory that would measure TAC overshoot, override, or
mixed-fishery constraint remains forthcoming.

Six Category~1 stocks are retained: Irish Sea cod
(\emph{Gadus morhua}, \texttt{cod.27.7a}), Irish Sea whiting
(\emph{Merlangius merlangus}, \texttt{whg.27.7a}), Celtic Sea cod
(\texttt{cod.27.7e-k}), Celtic Sea whiting
(\texttt{whg.27.7b-ce-k}), Celtic Sea haddock
(\emph{Melanogrammus aeglefinus}, \texttt{had.27.7b-k}), and Northeast
Atlantic mackerel (\emph{Scomber scombrus}, \texttt{mac.27.nea}).
Northeast Atlantic mackerel is a special case: a recent benchmark
involved a substantial change in the assessment, so the backtest
conflates following the advice with robustness of that advice to the
assessment change. It is retained for completeness and will be removed
from the final report. Advice
follows the ICES MSY approach except for mackerel, where the Coastal
States MSY rule applies. The stocks span a range of terminal status,
exploitation, and reference-point conventions, which is the point of the
common Historical / Current / Future template.

\subsection{Operating Models and stock--recruitment}
\label{sec:om}

Operating Models were conditioned on cleaned WGCSE \texttt{FLStock}
biology: time-varying weights, natural mortality, maturity and
selectivity as assessed. Six stock--recruitment relationship (SRR)
scenarios share that biology:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{bh1}} --- unconstrained Beverton--Holt
        \citep{beverton1957dynamics} fitted with
        \texttt{icesdata::eql(..., model = "bevholtSV")}. Steepness is
        \emph{estimated}, not fixed at~1.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{bh2}} --- the same Beverton--Holt form with a
        FishLife steepness prior.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{bh3}} --- Beverton--Holt with an $R_0$ prior
        taken from a hockey-stick fit.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{rk1}--\texttt{rk3}} --- Ricker
        \citep{ricker1954stock} analogues of the three Beverton--Holt
        scenarios.
\end{itemize}
This paper reports one scenario at a time, not the full Operating Model
set. Historical trajectories (open- and closed-loop histories) and the
20-year Future window are \texttt{bh3}. ICES HCR parameters stay at
published SAG values on \texttt{eqs} (\texttt{eqls[["bh1"]]}).
Operating Model evaluation reference points are \texttt{bh3}. Other
scenarios are forthcoming.

Recruitment deviations on Historical are that scenario's
\texttt{srResiduals(eq)}, replayed once. The iteration dimension is~1:
the run is deterministic, not a median of a stochastic ensemble. The
20-year Future window uses the same \texttt{bh3} Operating Model SRR
with \texttt{fwdWindow}, FLBRP mean biology, and recruitment deviations
of~1 (mean recruitment). Advice-rule Future starts from the
\texttt{bh3} closed-loop terminal state. It is longer than the ICES
1--2~year advice forecast and is not a stochastic forecast.

A segmented-regression (hockey-stick) form \citep{mesnil2010hockeystick}
is used only as the ICES HCR on the advice side, and as the source of
the \texttt{bh3} $R_0$ prior. It is not treated as Operating Model
``truth''.

\subsection{Harvest policies}
\label{sec:policies}

Three trajectories share the same Operating Model biology; only the
harvest policy differs. Open loop is a simple projection at constant
$F_{\mathrm{target}}$. Closed loop is an MSE with feedback control: the
ICES advice rule revises $F$ from SSB each year.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Historical} --- the latest WGCSE assessment
        (\texttt{FLStock}).
  \item $\boldsymbol{F}_{\mathrm{target}}$ --- \textbf{open loop}:
        simple projection; \texttt{fwd} at constant ICES SAG $\Fmsy$
        from 2006 to the terminal year, with that scenario's SRR residuals.
        No feedback: $F$ does not depend on estimated or true SSB. This
        is not the time-varying yearly advice-sheet $F$.
  \item \textbf{Advice rule (AR)} --- \textbf{closed loop}: MSE with
        feedback control; \texttt{hcrICES} from 2005 to the terminal
        year. The hockey-stick sets $F = \Ftar$ when SSB $\ge\Btrig$ and
        reduces $F$ towards $\Blim$ when SSB is below the trigger, so
        $F$ depends on biomass each year. A TAC is taken from a
        short-term \texttt{fwd} and applied as Operating Model catch.
        Settings are \texttt{err = NULL} (true Operating Model SSB),
        \texttt{implErr = 0}, and a one-year advice lag. The shortcut
        estimation-error branch is off.
\end{itemize}

The open-loop run is the no-feedback benchmark that isolates ``fish at
$\Fmsy$'' from ``follow the ICES HCR''. The two can disagree: Irish Sea
cod historical $F$ was about $0.07\times F_{\mathrm{target}}$, so fishing
\emph{at} $F_{\mathrm{target}}$ lowers SSB relative to history; Celtic
Sea stocks often sit below $\Btrig$, so the HCR fishes well below
$F_{\mathrm{target}}$.

ICES $\Ftar$, $\Blim$ and $\Btrig$ are taken from SAG
(\texttt{addBenchmarks} on \texttt{eqls[["bh1"]]}) and are the HCR
inputs (\texttt{hcrParams(eqs)}). They are held fixed across SRR
scenarios: in reality ICES does not re-derive advice parameters according
to which SRR happens to be true. Operating Model $\Fmsy$, $\Bmsy$ and
$R_0$ used for shading and evaluation are \texttt{refpts} from
\texttt{eqls[["bh3"]]}. Figure lines for $\Blim$, $\Btrig$ and
$F_{\mathrm{target}}$ remain the ICES SAG values on \texttt{eqs};
trajectories and Operating Model shading are the simulated
\texttt{bh3} scenario.

\subsection{ICES versus Operating Model reference points}
\label{sec:refpts}

Two roles for reference points are kept distinct.
ICES $\Ftar$, $\Blim$ and $\Btrig$ are \textbf{operational control
points} of the advice rule: they set $F$ each year and stay at published
SAG values. Operating Model $\Bmsy$ and $\Fmsy$ (\texttt{bh3}
equilibrium) and years to those levels are \textbf{performance metrics}:
they score whether the CFP/MSY objective is met on the assumed truth.
Reaching ICES $\Btrig$ does not imply Operating Model $\Bmsy$.

\citet{winker2026rebuilding} make the same split. The CFP objective is
to restore stocks above biomass levels that produce MSY, but the ICES
MSY framework has no biomass target: advice is built on $\Fmsy$ (EqSim,
then capped by $F_{P.05}$), $\Blim$, and $MSYB_{\mathrm{trigger}}$.
$\Bmsy$ is an implicit EqSim output --- SSB when fishing at $\Fmsy$ ---
and is not reported. $MSYB_{\mathrm{trigger}}$ is ``an operationalised
management threshold that triggers a cautious reduction in fishing
mortality'', not a target. In practice it is usually $B_{\mathrm{pa}}$
($\approx 1.4\Blim$), not the 5th percentile of $\Bmsy$
\citep{winker2026rebuilding,winker2025precautionary}. Using the trigger
as a surrogate for $\Bmsy$ therefore classifies stocks as ``at MSY''
while they are still rebuilding: \citet{winker2026rebuilding} find
$MSYB_{\mathrm{trigger}}$ is on average about 60\% of $\Bmsy$.

The hockey-stick (segmented regression) is how ICES usually
\emph{derives} those control points, not a biological production
hypothesis. The SRR is fitted after the assessment; $\Blim$ is typically
the hockey-stick breakpoint (recruitment impairment), and
$MSYB_{\mathrm{trigger}}$ is then a multiplier of $\Blim$. EqSim at
WKMSYREF almost exclusively used segmented regression
\citep{winker2026rebuilding}. After the breakpoint, expected recruitment
is a constant, so the production function is truncated: for many stocks
the breakpoint sits at or near $\Bmsy$, which would make a limit
essentially equal to the target. This paper therefore does not treat the
ICES hockey-stick as Operating Model truth. The HCR \emph{shape} remains
that hockey-stick; the Operating Model SRR is Beverton--Holt (\texttt{bh3}, $R_0$
prior from the hockey-stick breakpoint --- the same BH sensitivity
\citet{winker2026rebuilding} used when ICES does not supply $\Bmsy$).

The backtest therefore needs an Operating Model and a closed-loop run.
ICES reference points are not a property of one internally consistent
population. An Operating Model conditioned on the WGCSE \texttt{FLStock} gives
$\Bmsy$ and $\Fmsy$ from the same SRR used to project Historical and
Future, so years-to-$\Bmsy$ is comparable with the hindcast. EqSim as
used in ICES benchmarks is largely an open-loop simple projection at
constant $F$: it does not simulate the feedback control loop of MSE
(data, advice lag, HCR, catch implementation).
\citet{winker2025precautionary} showed that EqSim $\Fmsy$ is not
precautionary relative to a shortcut MSE on an Operating Model that retains
assessment structure and advice lags. That is why this study conditions
an Operating Model, keeps published ICES HCR parameters as control points, evaluates
rebuild against Operating Model MSY performance metrics, and contrasts a simple
open-loop projection at $F_{\mathrm{target}}$ with a closed-loop MSE
with feedback.

For the Irish and Celtic Sea gadoids, $\Btrig$ is the ICES SAG
\texttt{MSYBtrigger}. For Northeast Atlantic mackerel that SAG field is
not on the same published basis: it is a derived SAG
\texttt{MSYBtrigger}, equal to $B_{\mathrm{pa}}$ in the latest SAG row,
rather than the long-term management plan / $\Blim$ convention on the
ICES advice sheet. Mackerel ratios in the tables are therefore not
comparable, as published quantities, with the gadoid ratios.

\subsection{Historical, Current, Future}
\label{sec:framework}

\textbf{Historical} is the window from 2005 (Advice rule) or 2006 ($F_{\mathrm{target}}$) to
the terminal year, shown as trajectories of $F$, SSB, recruitment and
catch (Figure~\ref{fig:path-cod7a} and the remaining Historical figures). The
$F_{\mathrm{target}}$ line is ICES $\Ftar$ from SAG; $F$ shading is Operating Model
$\Fmsy$ (\texttt{bh3}). SSB is shaded below Operating Model $\Bmsy$
(\texttt{bh3}).

\textbf{Current} is terminal-year status:
$SSB/\Btrig$, absolute $\bar F$, and $\bar F/\Ftar$.
Table~\ref{tab:position} reports Historical, $F_{\mathrm{target}}$ and Advice rule,
with $\Delta$ defined as Advice-rule minus Historical
$SSB/\Btrig$. Under $F_{\mathrm{target}}$, $\bar F/\Ftar$ is 1 by construction.

\textbf{Future} is a 20-year HCR projection from each terminal state
on the \texttt{bh3} Operating Model, mean biology and mean recruitment.
Both the Historical and Advice-rule series use the ICES HCR after the
terminal year; differences are starting state, not a change of rule.
Rebuild time is the first projected year in which SSB reaches ICES
$\Btrig$ or Operating Model $\Bmsy$. Already above is recorded as
0~years; not reached within 20~years is recorded as missing.

Software: \texttt{FLCore}, \texttt{FLBRP}, \texttt{FLasher},
\texttt{FLBacktest} (\texttt{hcrICES}, \texttt{openloop\_start},
\texttt{ltermEq}, \texttt{project\_hcr}), \texttt{icesdata}, and
\texttt{ggplotFL}. Application notebooks and deliverables:
\url{https://github.com/laurieKell/backtest-ices}; generic engine:
\url{https://github.com/laurieKell/FLBacktest}.
The \texttt{Rmd/} knit chain is the supplementary methods
(\texttt{00\_supplement.Rmd}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Terminal status}
\label{sec:position}

All six stocks are below $\Btrig$ in the terminal assessment year
(Table~\ref{tab:position}). Realised $\bar F$ exceeds ICES $\Ftar$
except in Irish Sea cod. Constant $\Ftar$ rebuilds Irish Sea whiting
($SSB/\Btrig = 4.94$) and Celtic Sea cod ($4.22$) to several times the
trigger. The Advice rule sets $F = \Ftar$ once SSB is above the trigger
(about a one-year lag); the useful contrast is how far each stock sits
below the trigger, and how much SSB the Advice rule gains or loses
relative to history ($\Delta$ in Table~\ref{tab:position}).

Irish Sea whiting ($\Delta = 4.45$) and Celtic Sea cod ($\Delta = 4.21$)
are the large Advice-rule gains. Celtic Sea haddock ends above the
trigger under both counterfactuals ($\Delta = 0.58$). Celtic Sea whiting
improves modestly ($\Delta = 0.27$) and remains below the trigger.
Mackerel $SSB/\Btrig$ is unchanged at $0.77$ ($\Delta = 0.00$); the
Advice rule reduces $F$ ($F/\Ftar = 0.91$) because SSB is still below
the trigger. Irish Sea cod is the exception: both counterfactuals
\emph{lower} terminal SSB relative to history ($\Delta = -0.21$).

\subsection{Irish Sea cod}
\label{sec:cod7a}

Historical $\bar F$ is already well below $\Ftar$
($F/\Ftar \approx 0.07$), so fishing at $\Ftar$ lowers terminal SSB
relative to history (Table~\ref{tab:position}: $F_{\mathrm{target}}$ and Advice-rule
$SSB/\Btrig$ both below the Historical ratio). That is the opposite of
the other depleted gadoids, where the Advice rule would have cut $F$
(Figure~\ref{fig:path-cod7a}).

Two mechanisms can produce a realised $F$ this far below the ICES
target, and this backtest does not yet separate them. One is
mixed-fishery constraint: Irish Sea cod has been treated as a recovery /
choke stock, so catch can sit well below a single-stock TAC even when
the advice rule would allow $\Ftar$. The other is vintage: ICES $\Ftar$
is the SAG \texttt{FMSY} used as an HCR input, whereas Operating Model
$\Fmsy$ (\texttt{bh3} shading) is a different equilibrium quantity ---
for this stock the Operating Model value is higher than ICES $\Ftar$,
yet both are far above historical $\bar F$. An advised-catch trajectory
would distinguish a choke/recovery TAC from an Operating Model /
$\Ftar$ mismatch; it is not in this draft.

On the \texttt{bh3} Future run, Irish Sea cod reaches Operating Model
$\Bmsy$ \emph{before} ICES $\Btrig$ (6 versus 8~years from history;
7 versus 8~years from the Advice-rule start;
Table~\ref{tab:rebuild}, Figure~\ref{fig:prospects-cod7a}). That ordering
occurs because $\Bmsy$ sits below ICES $\Btrig$ on this scenario.
Rebuilding to the advice threshold and rebuilding to MSY biomass are
therefore not the same target.

\subsection{Celtic Sea whiting: $\Btrig$ versus $\Bmsy$}
\label{sec:whg}

Under the \texttt{bh3} Future run, Celtic Sea whiting never reaches
ICES $\Btrig$ in 20~years from either start, but it does reach
Operating Model $\Bmsy$ in 18~years from history and 16~years from the
Advice-rule start (Table~\ref{tab:rebuild},
Figure~\ref{fig:prospects-whg}). ICES $\Btrig$ sits materially above
Operating Model $\Bmsy$ on this scenario, so the advice threshold is not a
proxy for MSY biomass. A 20-year mean-recruitment HCR can look like a
successful rebuild against the Operating Model yardstick while remaining
``below trigger'' on the ICES scale.

On the \texttt{bh3} Historical run, constant $\Ftar$ raises terminal
$SSB/\Btrig$ only from 0.22 to 0.40, and the Advice rule to 0.49
(Table~\ref{tab:position}, Figure~\ref{fig:path-whg}). Realised
$F/\Ftar = 1.42$, so the HCR does cut $F$, but not by enough --- given
this SRR and these residuals --- to put the stock on the ICES trigger
within the historical window.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Implementation versus the advice rule}

The design question is whether poor terminal status is an implementation
failure, an advice-rule failure, or both. This draft does not yet answer
that question with an advised-catch counterfactual. What it does answer
is narrower and still useful.

$F_{\mathrm{target}}$ versus Historical asks what constant ICES $\Ftar$ would have
done, given the Operating Model biology and replayed recruitment
residuals. Advice rule versus $F_{\mathrm{target}}$ isolates the hockey-stick: $F$ is
reduced while SSB is below $\Btrig$, then returns to $\Ftar$ with a
one-year lag. Advice rule versus Historical mixes that HCR with whatever
caused realised $F$ to depart from $\Ftar$ --- TAC overshoot, mixed-fishery
constraint, assessment revision, or a mismatch between SAG $\Ftar$ and
Operating Model $\Fmsy$.

For Celtic Sea cod, Irish Sea whiting and Celtic Sea haddock, realised
$F$ was above $\Ftar$ and the Advice rule would have left the stock at
or above the trigger by the terminal year (Table~\ref{tab:position}).
That is the pattern in which following the rule would have changed
Current. For Celtic Sea whiting the same qualitative pattern holds, but
the gain is small and Future still never reaches ICES $\Btrig$
(Section~\ref{sec:whg}): cutting $F$ to the HCR is not sufficient, under
this SRR and mean recruitment, to satisfy the ICES threshold. For
mackerel, Historical $SSB/\Btrig$ does not move; the HCR trims $F$ because the
stock is below the SAG trigger, while Operating Model $\Bmsy$ is already
exceeded.

Irish Sea cod is the implementation contrast in the other direction.
Realised $F$ is far below the single-stock target, and imposing $\Ftar$
or the HCR \emph{reduces} terminal SSB. Calling that an implementation
failure would be a category error: catch sat below what a single-stock
Category~1 rule would have allowed. Whether that was a mixed-fishery
choke, a recovery TAC, or a reference-point mismatch is not identified
here. Until an advised-catch trajectory is run, implementation and the
advice rule remain only partly separated
\citep{punt2016msebestpractice,winker2026rebuilding}.

\subsection{Mackerel reference-point basis}

Northeast Atlantic mackerel is retained, but its $\Btrig$ ratios are not
on the same published basis as the gadoids. The SAG \texttt{MSYBtrigger}
used as an HCR input equals $B_{\mathrm{pa}}$ in the latest SAG row;
the advice sheet uses a long-term management plan / $\Blim$ convention.
A recent benchmark shifted both the stock trend and the reference-point
basis. The result that mackerel is already above Operating Model
$\Bmsy$ while still below the SAG trigger is therefore a statement about
those two numbers on this Operating Model, not a claim that ICES advice
and MSY biomass agree. Future rebuild times to $\Btrig$ (3~years)
should be read with that caveat.

\subsection{Structural uncertainty forthcoming}

SRR form is a discrete structural-uncertainty axis, not a process-error
replicate. This paper reports \texttt{bh3} on both Historical and Future;
ICES HCR parameters remain the published SAG values on \texttt{eqs}.
Figure lines for $\Blim$, $\Btrig$ and $F_{\mathrm{target}}$ are those
ICES inputs; Operating Model shading is \texttt{bh3}. \texttt{bh3} is
Beverton--Holt with an $R_0$ prior from a hockey-stick; it is not a
steepness-1 Beverton--Holt. \texttt{bh1} is unconstrained
\texttt{bevholtSV} with estimated steepness. Scenarios still to be reported
are \texttt{bh2} (FishLife steepness prior) and the Ricker analogues
\texttt{rk1}--\texttt{rk3}. HCR inputs will remain at published ICES
values across those scenarios; Operating Model $\Bmsy$ and $\Fmsy$ will
be recalculated per scenario.

This draft is deterministic (residuals replayed once; Future at mean
recruitment). It does not propagate estimation error, does not refit
SAM, and does not use a shortcut assessment emulator. Those are
subsequent labelled axes, not results claimed here.
